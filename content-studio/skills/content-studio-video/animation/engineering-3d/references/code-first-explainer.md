# Code-first 3D explainer

Use the [Animated Video form](../../../../../prompts/animated-video.md). Keep authored Blender Python, asset parameters, cue data, and render settings in the project. Generate geometry programmatically or import approved meshes from code. In code-first scope, reproduce changes and renders from the project’s command line.

## Workflow in order

1. **Analyze the source.** Deliver `script-analysis.md` with timed narration beats, visible parts, object action, camera action, and evidence. With a video reference, inspect complete action sequences around short or ambiguous moves. For a new topic, establish the teaching beats and use real photos plus documentation or measured motion for the mechanism. **Human review** — Confirm the mechanism and evidence. Stop at this handoff for analysis-only.
2. **Draft the script and gather asset evidence in parallel.** After the applicable precheck, write original narration while collecting the exact model and revision, measured dimensions, assembled and exploded views, moving contacts, and the visible states that the narration depends on. Record unknown geometry or timing. Label illustrative internals and data paths on screen. Combine both tracks into the timed storyboard.
   - Use one stable beat ID and stable part IDs per action. Record the absolute beat range and a cue phrase or timestamp for each change. For each moving part record its parent or anchor, pivot or axis, starting and final states, and what stays attached. Give the camera its own move and hold. Include a visible pass condition. Use estimated times until narration is aligned.
   - **Human review · Script and storyboard** — Review the narration, exact model and planned parts, visible sequence, and accuracy limits. Stop at this handoff for script-and-storyboard scope. For builds, confirm the current production plan and selected commands before implementing.
3. **Lock cues and build parts.** After the script and production plan are approved, align a scratch read while building meshes independently. Parameterize dimensions, stable object names, units, transforms, pivots, materials and assembly states. Preserve the parent transform when the camera changes. Generate repeated parts from one definition. Time custom action, camera, and labels from the same resolved cue sheet. Save the inputs, editable scene source and reproducible command.
4. **Prove the assembly and action.** Render assembled views with angles matched to real photos. Use a fixed camera to test one complete mechanism from start through motion to settled state. Inspect contact, clearance and pivots. Then render a short narrated proof with the intended camera and callouts at delivery size. **Human review · Scene proof** — Inspect matched stills, full-speed playback and scores. Approve accuracy, visible motion and cue timing before scaling the treatment. Fix and rerender the bounded shot first.
5. **Assemble and verify.** Carry only reviewed renders and audio forward. Recheck actual narration onset, held results, assembly states, and important joins in the exact master. **Human review · Exact master** — Review the export and record its hash plus the checklist evidence. Resume with packaging when the master is approved.

## Choose the Blender route

- **Built-in method · `blender-cutaway`.** The JSON renderer supports component-wide separation or assembly, opacity and highlight events, and orthographic camera position, look target and scale. It accepts GLB, glTF and FBX imports as named components. Use authored separated parts for cutaways. See the [working Blender command and example](../previews/example-scene.md).
- **Project Blender Python.** Script staged transforms, rotor or joint animation, section geometry, custom materials, data paths and complex camera work in the project. Specify the headless entry point and arguments in the production plan and verify them with a bounded proof. The built-in JSON renderer has no events for arbitrary component transforms or mesh sectioning.

For a project-authored entry point, use `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python <entry.py> -- <project-arguments>`. Define explicit input and output paths and a bounded shot or frame range. Check the expected resolution, frame count, and object IDs before accepting a generated proof.

## Take proofs into a long render

- Synthesize or record complete thoughts. Log the actual spoken cue positions inside the read, then drive scene events from those positions. Splitting at every visual cue can pause a sentence in the middle. Recheck timing after padding, trimming, or time-stretching.
- Sample each shot at its initial state, main action, and held result with the [headless preview tool](headless-preview.md). Check screen-space labels through lens changes, legibility over lit geometry, and an underside or reverse view when it contains evidence that matters.
- Store independently verified picture segments at one resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate and time base. Match source hashes before reuse. Keep one aligned narration master across all picture segments, then assemble and verify exact frame count and audio duration. Batch image-sequence storage per shot when the whole video would exceed working disk space.
- Inspect the frame before and after each cut in the encoded master. Score the exact voice, lesson, model, and motion separately from the first proof.

## Critique a 3D render

1. **Check physical accuracy first.** Use [photo matching](../../../review/critique/references/photo-reference.md) for the same model, view and assembled state. Check proportions, part count and placement, material, depth and visibility. Verify physical motion against real footage, photos of ordered states or technical documentation. Leave unsupported engineering details open, even if the shot is readable.
2. **Check the mechanism in motion.** Map the same action independently in reference and candidate. Use real-speed playback and [consecutive frames](../../../review/critique/references/frame-tracing.md) around reveal, movement, contact and hold. Inspect a fixed-camera proof first and then the same shot with the camera and narration. Screen-space tracks help locate events and serve as timing evidence. Author actual part transforms from known geometry, axes and constraints.
3. **Score and retest.** Use the [object rubric and scorer](../../../review/critique/references/object-scoring.md) on physical parts and illustrative actors that teach the mechanism. Record comparable stills or playback for both sides, plus the six appearance and motion axes. Judge schematic form by clarity and correctness. Log each gap as beat ID, part ID, time, code or parameter to change, and evidence expected on retest. Resolve factual or mechanical errors even if the aggregate score passes.
4. **Check the lesson.** Run [teaching scoring](../../../review/critique/references/teaching-scoring.md) on the exact proof and again on the narrated master. Set the intended audience and keep identify, predict, and explain checks stable across revisions. Review the learner checks with any observed answers and keep untested comprehension open.
5. **Score the package.** Use [artifact scoring](../../../review/critique/references/artifact-scoring.md) with the generated script, storyboard, heroes, animation, narration, mix, source and final export. Inspect the exact files. Keep absent or pending items visible at each checkpoint.
