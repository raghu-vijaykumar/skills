# Puzzle platformer review

The [original Blender builder](examples/puzzle_platformer.py) stages a three-platform jump puzzle. A blue jumper clears a measured 0.70 m first gap, lands on a pressure pad, extends a cyan bridge across a measured 1.00 m second gap, and reaches a gold goal. Red four-sided spikes mark both fall zones. The [pinned upstream puzzle platformer direction](https://github.com/arjun988/blender-skills/blob/904f095d8f36a6e72adaebb08d07f884a074743d/.claude/skills/genre-puzzle-platformer/SKILL.md) calls for clear interactables, distinct hazard shape and color, and honest jump distances in the mesh. This is an authored visual sequence, not playable collision or input logic.

Both complete Blender 5.3 candidates passed the shot contract at **72 frames, 960×540, 24 fps**. The [scorebook](scores.json) records **v1 63/100** (subject 4, motion 1, composition 4, finish 3, evidence 5) and **v2 78/100** (4, 4, 4, 3, 5). V1's MP4 SHA-256 is `4f9d1ce6f663c9857909586845a085671f36649f7ed82c03601cee649e9e2b79`; its GIF SHA-256 is `46db72f096d3b05736fd355c70f7e989e726e8f5864c60e27103de9d02f24f36`. The puzzle layout reads, but the jumper stays near the start and the bridge remains closed.

The [selected v2 GIF](previews/puzzle-platformer.gif) shows a jump arc, center-pad arrival, bridge extension, and goal arrival. Its MP4 SHA-256 is `588d925fe3234d5042f4687ef698633e20f6d516bfd14da892a115939b8e02c8`; its GIF SHA-256 is `6da7b4468c67ac8df6e4c6362b26c78441fdfb952e97f72f2e3c6a6679be9c7e`. Both GIFs decode as 24 frames at 480×270; the rejected output remains in ignored `tmp/`.

The jumper, switch, bridge, and goal were previewed alone before full rendering. Encoded MP4 frames at the start, jump, switch, and final state, plus both GIFs' final frames, were inspected. Both saved `.blend` files passed the [structural audit](scripts/audit_puzzle.py): mesh-measured gap widths, five red spike meshes distinct from the cyan switch material, parented jumper geometry, and exact keyed jump and bridge poses. Small spike silhouettes and simple character geometry limit finish; the switch and goal are visual symbols rather than gameplay systems.
