# Cosmic aperture review

The [original builder](examples/cosmic_aperture.py) stages an eight-meter broken aperture above a small witness. Nine misregistered floor slabs, eight floating monoliths, and nine detached orbit shards make the architecture feel spatially wrong. An unshown void, abyssal blue stone, sickly gold seams, two partial tendrils, and sparse glints follow the [pinned upstream cosmic direction](https://github.com/arjun988/blender-skills/blob/904f095d8f36a6e72adaebb08d07f884a074743d/.claude/skills/cosmic-eldritch-horror/SKILL.md). The geometry and animation are original. This is one authored cosmic horror shot, not a general eldritch creature generator.

Three full Blender 5.3 candidates contain 72 frames at 960×540 and 24 fps. The [scorebook](scores.json) records **v1 63/100** (subject 3, motion 2, composition 4, finish 3, evidence 5), **v2 73/100** (4, 3, 4, 3, 5), and **v3 78/100** (4, 4, 4, 3, 5). The aperture was previewed alone and in its full stage. Exact encoded opening, middle, and final frames and the selected GIF were inspected. All three saved `.blend` scenes passed the [structural audit](scripts/audit_cosmic.py): the aperture is about 7.96 times the witness height, the broken geometry and camera settings are present, and the candidate-specific iris motion exists. Resumable full renders passed shot-contract, MP4 frame, and resolution checks. The result uses suggestive architecture rather than a fully modeled monster; its dark background and clean concentric forms limit the horror finish score.
