# Wasteland fuel stop review

The [original Blender builder](examples/wasteland_station.py) chooses one post-apocalyptic roadside lane: a broken fuel-stop canopy held by an improvised brace, oxidized repairs, a faded fictional sign, a dead pump, blowing dust, and a salvaged four-wheel hauler. The [pinned upstream apocalypse-worlds direction](https://github.com/arjun988/blender-skills/blob/904f095d8f36a6e72adaebb08d07f884a074743d/.claude/skills/apocalypse-worlds/SKILL.md) calls for collapse language through scavenged materials, faded brands, and improvised repairs. This original scene demonstrates one wasteland lane, not zombie, alien, or post-human settings.

Both complete Blender 5.3 candidates have 72 frames at 960×540 and 24 fps. The hauler was previewed alone and then in the full roadside composition. Its first isolated preview exposed a floating tarp; four visible supports were added before either full render. Exact encoded opening, middle, and final MP4 frames and both GIFs' final poses were inspected. Both saved `.blend` files passed the [structural audit](scripts/audit_wasteland.py): damaged and braced canopy, five rust roof patches, a three-part fictional sign mark, dead pump and hose, four cart wheels and supports, three repair plates, and 18 moving dust streaks. Both full renders passed the shot contract and exact encoded-frame checks. The station and cart are simplified stylized geometry; the dust is a designed visual sweep rather than a particle simulation.
