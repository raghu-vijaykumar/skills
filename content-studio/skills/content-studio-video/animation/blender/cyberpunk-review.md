# Cyberpunk courier alley review

The [original Blender builder](examples/cyberpunk_alley.py) chooses one sci-fi/punk lane: a cyan and magenta cyberpunk transit alley with a parcel drone. The [pinned upstream sci-fi/punk direction](https://github.com/arjun988/blender-skills/blob/904f095d8f36a6e72adaebb08d07f884a074743d/.claude/skills/sci-fi-punk-worlds/SKILL.md) says to choose one lane instead of mixing chrome cyberpunk with brass steampunk. This example does not claim hard sci-fi, solarpunk, or the other listed lanes.

Both complete Blender 5.3 candidates have 72 frames at 960×540 and 24 fps. The [scorebook](scores.json) records **v1 64/100** (subject 4, motion 2, composition 4, finish 2, evidence 5) and **v2 74/100** (4, 4, 4, 2, 5). The drone barely moves in v1.

The [selected v2 GIF](previews/cyberpunk-alley.gif) scores higher because the parcel drone crosses the alley while the camera advances toward the suspended sign. Its MP4 SHA-256 is `9bc3e2cf4afa641204d8a70741dfbac177884d1cf6e4968279a659df9cfefbec`; its GIF SHA-256 is `b8a39bcaf7fb52b1ad58ce8be670e152f80722797f16f5816c50335a4d634d0e`. Both GIFs decode as 24 frames at 480×270. The rejected output remains in ignored `tmp/`.

The drone was previewed alone, then in the full alley composition. An initial camera angle left half the frame empty; the corrected centerline camera was used for both full renders. Exact encoded opening, middle, and final MP4 frames and both GIFs' final poses were inspected. Both saved `.blend` files passed the [structural audit](scripts/audit_cyberpunk.py): 12 tower bays, 12 neon spines, 36 window rails, a suspended glyph sign, four drone rotors, and the parcel attached to the drone rig. In v2 the drone moves from x=-3.0 m to x=2.25 m while the camera advances from y=-11.4 m to y=-9.7 m. Both full renders passed the shot contract and exact encoded-frame checks. The facades are simple and the low-roughness street has weak visible reflections; this is a stylized lane demonstration, not a high-fidelity rain or urban simulation.
