# Retro 8-bit ship review

The [original sprite builder](examples/retro_8bit_ship.py) stages one arcade spaceship, one projectile, an asteroid, and four impact fragments. Every sprite is editable square-pixel mesh geometry, with four emission-only palette colors, a locked 320×240 orthographic camera, 12 fps, and held location keys. The scene has no gradients or lights. This is one authored animation, not a general 8-bit scene generator or an NES hardware emulator.

Two full Blender 5.3 MP4s contain 48 frames each at 320×240. Six GIF encodes of those outputs were scored in the [scorebook](scores.json). Automatic four-color quantization lost green and orange (51/100 for v1, 60/100 for v2); the eight-color probe also merged green with cream (73/100), and the sixteen-color probe added extra encoded edge colors (78/100). All four encoding probes failed the intended four-color GIF check. Their exact GIF hashes and review reasons are retained in the scorebook. The fixed-palette **v1 scored 73/100** (subject 4, motion 3, composition 3, finish 4, evidence 5) and **v2 scored 82/100** (4, 4, 4, 4, 5). V1's MP4 SHA-256 is `0e3d031f76b59e8f26704865c315ecdac58d8570f5e0fedcd5f7ef42b0fe28b1`; its fixed-palette GIF SHA-256 is `81a60444c9984bd4bacdb2878a20d5de27b17251e2d09b524821bd16d5f7b255`. Its impact fragments stayed close to the asteroid's original silhouette.

The [selected v2 GIF](previews/retro8-ship.gif) uses a wider, still in-frame four-way split. Its MP4 SHA-256 is `ef8151eb3840bf532f543d994ca022dee2739c7186a2d86dc8bdb3536b589955` and GIF SHA-256 is `549173b3aa5ee96657008cf2fe1ec19430780ae5144c7211ebdfa5362a8773e3`. Both GIFs decode as 24 frames at 320×240. The selected GIF was quantized from the exact MP4 with a fixed four-color palette sampled from that render. The rejected output remains in ignored `tmp/`.

Exact encoded approach, projectile, and split frames were inspected. Both editable `.blend` files passed the [structural audit](scripts/audit_retro8.py): four emission-only fills, square planar sprite faces, no lights, a fixed low-resolution camera, 48-frame shot, held location keys, and correct reveal timing. The selected v2 GIF has exactly four colors across its frames. [Pinned upstream 8-bit guidance](https://github.com/arjun988/blender-skills/blob/904f095d8f36a6e72adaebb08d07f884a074743d/.claude/skills/retro-8bit-style/SKILL.md) informed the severe palette, high contrast, and sprite logic; the scene and motion are original.
