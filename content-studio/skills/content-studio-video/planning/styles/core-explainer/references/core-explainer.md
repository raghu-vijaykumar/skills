# Core Explainer

Current built-in formats: video, carousel, post. The active registry is [styles.ts](../../../../animation/videogen/runtime/styles.ts); design tokens remain in [videogen runtime](../../../../animation/videogen/runtime/design/). Use [video guidance](../../../../animation/videogen/references/video.md) for authored scenes and [posts guidance](../../../../animation/videogen/references/posts.md) for content packages. Choose and review a visual representation for each teaching beat.

For a narrated mechanism that benefits from kinetic type, a visible chapter path, or an exploded system view, import `KineticHeadline`, `ChapterSpine`, or `LayeredStack` from `@raghu-vijaykumar/videogen/runtime/components/motion-lab/index`. Cue them from the video timeline and choose each beat's object, palette, and framing separately. These are source-neutral Remotion components, not a fixed Stripe treatment. The private Stripe motion-lab probe exercised them at 0–15 s; its simplified 3D and device art did not meet the reference's visual quality, so inspect each hero at delivery size before extending it to a full Core Explainer.

For an agent-authored idea-to-video route, use the [scene plan contract](../../../../animation/videogen/references/idea-to-explainer.md) and `ExplainerFilm` from `@raghu-vijaykumar/videogen/runtime/motionExplainer`. The reusable scene types cover hero, device, flow, stack, code, data, document, and presenter beats in either aspect ratio. These are starting representations; build custom geometry and interfaces when a mechanism needs a more exact visual. Record semantic state changes, camera motion, original asset provenance, cue alignment, and native-frame review.

When an illustrated presenter carries an explanation, use the [2D rig workflow](../../../../animation/videogen/scene-authoring/references/presenter-rig.md). Build cartoons and caricatures of specific people from photos of those people and review likeness in the intended style. Key gaze and wrist targets to the object that matters in each beat. Review the hands and acting in isolation and then with the word, camera, and hero motion that motivate the gesture.

Start with the audience question and show a cause, an action and its result. Sync the decisive word to the corresponding state change where it improves understanding. Hold the starting state, open a detail view for the moving part, and hold the result long enough to read it. A restrained palette can keep attention on the active part. Reuse the same [production and critique workflow](../../../../review/critique/references/reconstruction.md), rigs and cue clock as Rapid Take, with pacing chosen for the mechanism.

When the explanation compares two routes or a changing set, draw both from the same underlying states. Keep a route's origin, payload and destination visible through a branch. When part of a production line goes idle, let its individual objects stop and derive any count or graph from that state. In the narrated render, verify the shared starting state, the point of divergence and the settled comparison with [paired frame landmarks](../../../../review/critique/references/frame-tracing.md).

For teaching results that appear as a number, status, or decision, give the result its own readable hold after movement settles. Preserve the originating actor as context and use the exact export to inspect the beginning, middle and final frames of the hold. Choose the hold from the amount of information the viewer must read.

When a creative reference sets the craft target, use the [object scoring rubric](../../../../review/critique/references/object-scoring.md) for hero assets, faces and supporting props. Compare form, material treatment and staging separately from mechanics, timing and continuity. Rerender and score the affected actors after each revision.

For an original topic without a reference video, [build and score heroes from real-world photos](../../../../review/critique/references/photo-reference.md). Use photos for physical form and finish. Use observed states or documentation for the moving assembly and inspect the whole action in the rendered cut.
